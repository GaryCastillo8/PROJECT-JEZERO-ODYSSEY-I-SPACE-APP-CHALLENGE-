03 · Mission question

What did water leave behind?

Read the changing environment

Follow evidence from crater-floor rocks to delta remnants and the western fan toe: igneous textures, sedimentary layers, contacts, and possible alteration minerals. Jezero preserves inlet channels and deposits interpreted as an ancient lake-and-delta system. [2][3]

Choose samples by context

Prioritize fine-grained outcrops and unit boundaries to test for clays and carbonates. Perseverance confirmed olivine/pyroxene and localized carbonate alteration in specific Séítah targets; do not assume those minerals occur at every station. [2][3][4]

05 · Evidence and uncertainty

Observed, interpreted, and hypothetical

Observed

Igneous rocks and sediments occur in the rover field; olivine/pyroxene and carbonates are documented in specific rock targets. Do not generalize those detections to every mapped unit. [3]

Interpretation

The western fan and the Pilot Pinnacle/Kodiak buttes are interpreted as remnants of the fluvial-deltaic-lacustrine system; their depositional architecture remains a field question. [3][11]

Ancient water

Neretva Vallis and other channels are part of the reconstructed ancient hydrologic system. RIMFAX surveyed subsurface structure across the floor-to-delta transition; this is not evidence of liquid water today. [2][4]

Ice: hypothesis

No ice-sampling stop is assigned. A published stability model finds shallow ground ice would be unstable today at Jezero; no confirmed shallow ice deposit is mapped along this route. Treat deeper ice as an open question, not a resource. [9]

06 · Mars environment

Constraints on movement and operations

Mean gravity
3.73 m/s² · about 0.38 g
Lower weight does not make travel easy; balance, suit mobility, inertia, and fall recovery still matter. [7]
Surface pressure
6.36 mbar mean; varies with season and elevation
Extremely thin, non-breathable atmosphere dominated by CO₂. Surface activity requires a pressurized suit and life support. [7][15]
Temperature
Global mean ~214 K (−59 °C); −89 to −31 °C range reported at Viking 1
The cited range is not a Jezero-specific observation. Schedule only after local environmental and suit limits are established. [7]
Radiation
RAD measured ~210 μGy/day absorbed dose during its initial surface period
Do not convert directly to human effective dose; solar particle events remain a major uncertainty. [8]
Dust
Local, regional, and global dust storms occur
Visibility, abrasion, filters, and power are concerns. No weather forecast is assumed for a future mission date. [7][15]
Local relief
Crater floor, boulders, ripples, and a western-delta scarp
The delta scarp is reported roughly 70–90 m above the landing-site elevation. This concept stops at the toe and avoids climbing it; the DTM has not been used for operational slope acceptance. [3][14]
07 · Hazard controls

Design rules before any EVA

Hazard
Potential consequence
Conceptual control
Depressurization / atmosphere
Life-threatening exposure; ambient atmosphere is not breathable.
Pressurized mobility, suit checks, buddy procedures, shelter, and return margin. No unsuited walk is proposed.
Cold and power
Thermal stress and equipment/battery limits.
Set sortie windows only after local weather and suit/system limits are available.
Radiation / solar event
Cumulative exposure plus difficult-to-predict solar particle events.
Dosimetry, solar monitoring, a shielded refuge, and a mission-level cancellation authority; no dose limit is invented here. [8]
Dust storm / visibility
Reduced visibility, contamination, abrasion, and mechanism wear.
Return to a pressurized refuge; cancel surface work when validated program thresholds are exceeded.
Blocks, ripples, slope
Falls, loss of traction, suit damage, or loss of a safe return path.
Review co-registered DTM, slope, roughness, and high-resolution imagery before selecting a real corridor. [14]
Delta scarp / loose sediment
Steep faces and unstable material at the fan front.
Remain on a verified low-gradient toe; remote observation first; do not ascend the scarp in this concept. [3]
08 · Operational sequence

How a future campaign could use the route

Before surface sorties

  1. Co-register CTX/HiRISE imagery, the USGS geologic map, and DTM products in a Mars planetary coordinate system.
  2. Review each station at higher resolution; the CTX map is not enough to assess individual boulders or suit-scale obstacles.
  3. Set weather, energy, communications, life-support, and turnaround rules with the actual habitat and vehicle design.

On the surface

  1. Start and finish at OEB, with shelter, communications, sample handling, and medical support at the base.
  2. Prioritize stops 02–05; observe the delta scarp from its toe instead of climbing it. Divide the loop into sorties according to validated limits.
  3. Record context before sampling and separate confirmed measurements from hypotheses.

GIS hand-off

The waypoint CSV accompanies this report. Use Mars 2000 / equirectangular coordinates with a 3,396,190 m sphere and east longitude 0–360°. Do not assign terrestrial WGS84. NASA Mars Trek documents the WMTS service and map grid. [1]

  1. Import CSV points with X = east longitude 0–360 and Y = planetocentric north latitude.
  2. Load USGS geologic map SIM 3464, the CTX mosaic, and the 20 m CTX DTM. Check datum and registration before combining products. [2][12][14]
  3. Compute slope, roughness, and terrain profiles; replace approximate station centers with validated feature coordinates.
  4. Only then create an operational-width corridor, alternatives, distance, and exposure estimate.

Coordinate note: The CSV contains planetary coordinates, not Earth-referenced GeoJSON. Stations 02–06 are explicitly marked as derived, digitized, or design locations.

09 · Perseverance context

A rover traverse is not a human route

NASA/JPL report that Perseverance had driven 45.22 km by 11 October 2026, exceeding Opportunity’s off-world driving record; that distance accumulated over roughly 5½ years. [5][10] This proposal is a much shorter return loop: about 9.4 km as straight geodesic segments between six station centers. The scales, support systems, and operating tempo are not directly comparable.

The official animation page and NASA location map are linked above. The YouTube player loaded only its opening frame in this session, so I could not verify the complete animation or extract a georeferenced rover polyline. To avoid inventing geometry, no Perseverance track is drawn on the map. The comparison is limited to NASA’s reported distance and the distinct purpose of this short human-reconnaissance concept; exact visual track comparison awaits a verified vector path. [5][6]

The USGS map and rover studies support the site science; this proposed traverse geometry, terrain safety, and approximate coordinates require independent GIS validation. [2][3][14]
